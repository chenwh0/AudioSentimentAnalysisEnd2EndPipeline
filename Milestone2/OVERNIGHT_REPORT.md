# Overnight report — Milestone 2 T1 final, T2 variants, WSP-Whisper and AudioXAI (run of 2026-11-18, 04:27–08:20 CST)

For a reader who knows machine learning but not this project. The project: a two-stage pipeline that transcribes short celebrity-interview clips (VoxCeleb, via the SLUE benchmark) with a speech-to-text model and then classifies the transcript's sentiment (negative / neutral / positive) with a text model. Milestone 1 compared model pairs; Milestone 2 turns the evaluation into a reproducible harness, adds model variants, and asks why the choice of speech-to-text model barely changes the sentiment result. Every number here is read from a file in the repository; the file is named next to each number. The per-phase reports hold the full tables: `Milestone2/T1_final/T1_REPORT.md`, `Milestone2/T2_variants/T2_REPORT.md`, `Milestone2/WSP_Whisper/results/wsp_results.md`, `Milestone2/AudioXAI/README.md`.

Run facts: one workstation GPU (RTX 4000 Ada, 20 GB); branch `m2-overnight` from `main` at `e2a81e44736fc03378f6123ec6c03c93d56c2333`; time box shortened from the brief's 10 hours (brief.md in the launch folder, not in this repo) to a push at 08:20 CST, so Phases 3 and 4 ran at reduced scope (`OVERNIGHT_STATUS.md`, `logs/ledger.md`).

## Phase 1 — the evaluation harness (DONE)

**Result.** Human transcripts for every clip were obtained from the SLUE benchmark and joined to all 3,426 clips with no label disagreement (`Milestone2/T1_final/results/gold_checks.json`). Against those transcripts, the Whisper-family speech-to-text models make 18–22% word errors (AventIQ 0.1831, whisper-tiny 0.2206; the s2t model 0.598) and the earlier "reference" machine transcript (whisper-large-v3) itself makes 17.9% (`results/wer_test.csv`). Feeding the human transcript to the best sentiment model (Cardiff) gives macro-F1 0.637 against 0.603–0.621 from machine transcripts (`results/m2_t1_results.csv`): perfect transcription would add at most 0.034 macro-F1, so the sentiment classifier, not the transcript, is the bottleneck. The always-neutral baseline scores accuracy 0.736 and macro-F1 0.283 (`results/baseline.json`); every Cardiff configuration on machine transcripts beats it on macro-F1 and none on accuracy (best 0.718); only Cardiff on human gold text edges past it at 0.739 (`results/m2_t1_results.csv`).

**Evidence.**
- The s2t model's reported 0.93 word error rate (against the machine pseudo-reference; `results/wer_test.csv`, row `s2t-small-OLD(batch8)`) was partly a measurement artefact: running it one clip at a time instead of eight at once cut looping transcripts from 244 to 134 of 1,500 (`results/s2t_loop_analysis.json`) and WER against human transcripts from 0.864 to 0.598 (`results/wer_test.csv`). A controlled probe on 48 development clips shows the cause is batch padding (11 of 48 transcripts loop at batch 8, 5 at batch 1, 38 with no attention mask), not a missing attention mask (`results/s2t_bug_probe.json`).
- Assertions, i.e. label-free checks that can run on every request, are now code (`Milestone2/T1_final/pipeline/assertions.py`): at least two words (A1), a plausible speaking rate (A2), classifier score above a development-tuned threshold (A3), and two new ones: agreement with a second classifier (A4a) and an audio-quality check from an acoustic degradation detector (A4b). A3 is useless for the two binary sentiment models, whose confidence is 0.99 whether right or wrong, as Milestone 1's calibration table predicted; the threshold rule returns 0.05 and passes every clip (`results/thresholds.json`). A4a is the check that works for every model: among clips that pass A1, A2, A3 and A4a together, accuracy is 0.60–0.75; among clips that fail that joint check, 0.10–0.74 (`results/assertions_test.csv`, columns `acc_pass_A123_A4a` and `acc_fail_A123_A4a`; for A4a alone the ranges are 0.59–0.72 and 0.07–0.81).
- Across configurations the pass rate still does not track macro-F1 (Pearson −0.24 with A1–A3, −0.08 with A4a, `results/correlations.json`): thresholds are tuned per configuration, so pass rates are not comparable between configurations.
- Every table is regenerated by `Milestone2/T1_final/run_t1.py` from cached transcripts; a fresh-context auditor reran it and recomputed every headline number (`logs/audit_phase1.md`: 8 of 9 items passed; the ninth was two wrong numbers and one missing section in the report text, all corrected).

**Limitations.** SLUE's transcribed span is shorter than the audio file by more than 0.1 s on 25.5% of clips (edge silence trimmed; never longer); WER on span-matched clips is lower for every model (`results/wer_test_spanmatched.csv`). Timing columns are offline GPU batch averages, not service latency.

## Phase 2 — model variants and registry (DONE)

**Result.** Six speech-to-text variants (five models plus the weight-edited `whisper-tiny-wsp` from Phase 3) and four sentiment variants are registered with pinned revisions, weight checksums, sizes of the files a service actually loads, and test-slice accuracy (`Milestone2/T2_variants/registry/`). The new Moonshine-base model has the lowest word error rate against human transcripts (0.161) but the lowest Cardiff macro-F1 (0.603); the Whisper variants (0.182–0.221 WER) give 0.615–0.621 (`Milestone2/T2_variants/results/wer_test.csv`, `m2_t1_results.csv`). Transcript accuracy and sentiment accuracy are not ordered the same way, which is the first half of the answer to "why doesn't the speech model matter".

**Evidence.** Grid of 6 × 4 = 24 configurations with 95% bootstrap CIs (`Milestone2/T2_variants/results/m2_t1_results.csv`); every pair of speech-to-text variants overlaps for Cardiff. AventIQ's model is a fine-tune of whisper-base whose weights differ by at most 0.0023 and whose results differ by 0.003 (`registry/asr/AventIQ/*/meta.json`). The sizes the earlier notebook reported (e.g. SiEBERT 4,073 MB) summed every weight format in the Hugging Face repository; a service loads one (1,423 MB) (`Milestone2/T2_variants/T2_REPORT.md`). MinIO was not reachable on this machine, so the registry is local (`registry/minio_attempt.json`).

**Limitations.** No quantized or ONNX variants; no latency numbers (course VM only).

## Phase 3 — WSP-Whisper, weight-space editing without training (PARTIAL)

**Result.** Following the weight-space-programming protocol (edit weights by reasoning about what they compute, measure with a small budget of forward passes, no gradients), whisper-tiny's registered checkpoint `fp17` rescales 432 rows of the decoder's token-embedding matrix (0.44% of the weights, 165,888 values) and lowers the test word error rate from 0.2206 to 0.2152, a paired change of −0.0054 with a 95% CI of [−0.0079, −0.0026] (`Milestone2/WSP_Whisper/results/wsp_results.csv`). It also lowers Cardiff macro-F1 from 0.6150 to 0.6086 (−0.0064, CI [−0.0119, −0.0016]). The WER gain is a style match to the human transcripts (numbers spelled out, colloquial forms kept), not better acoustics: WER against the whisper-large-v3 machine transcript rises from 0.1988 to 0.2100. The phase is PARTIAL: 17 of the 50 allowed forward passes were used (the night's cap was 20), only the fp10 and fp17 checkpoints exist, and the brief's floor for declaring a null result (40 passes and 3 hypothesis families, brief.md section 6) was not reached.

**Evidence.** Splits of the 500 development clips into 250 practice and 250 holdout clips with SHA-256 manifest (`Milestone2/WSP_Whisper/splits/manifest.json`); a locked evaluation harness whose hash is recorded (`wsp/HARNESS_LOCK.txt`); the base model's single test run reproduces the teammate's 0.1988 exactly (`results/eval_base_test.csv`); every forward pass logged with its hypothesis (`logs/forward_passes.jsonl`, 17 lines); every edit's hypothesis, old and new values and outcome (`edits/edit_log.jsonl`); sparse patches rebuilt by `wsp/apply_edits.py`; the registration written before any edited checkpoint touched the test slice (`results/REGISTERED.txt`, 05:25:49, orchestrator commit 303b9e7 at 05:37). The failed intermediate checkpoint fp10 (filler rows scaled up, which sends 18 test clips into "uh-uh-uh" loops; WER 0.357) is kept in the table. The output projection shares storage with the token embedding, so every row edit changes both a token's input embedding and its output logit (`results/layer_audit.csv`, `results/edit_locations.csv`).

**Limitations.** Two of the keep/revert decisions (E1 and E8) used holdout evaluation runs rather than practice passes, which the brief allows but which biases holdout numbers slightly (`logs/agents/W1_wsp.md`, status 05:20; holdout numbers are not reported as results). A reverted probe (`cand_E2E5`) scored 9 errors lower on holdout than fp17 but was never saved as a checkpoint or tested. The protocol was designed for decoder-only language models; its transfer to an encoder-decoder speech model is itself an open question.

## Phase 4 — AudioXAI: does the speech model attend to degraded audio? (PARTIAL, scope reduced)

**Result.** No. On a seeded stratified sample of 50 test clips (extended to 100), whisper-tiny's input attribution falls on degraded audio in proportion to its duration, not more: the mean focus ratio is 0.991 with a 95% CI of [0.911, 1.061] (n = 34 clips with at least one flagged frame; 16 clips have no flagged frame and 16 are flagged throughout), so the user's hypothesis H1 is refuted (`Milestone2/AudioXAI/results/summary.json`). Transcription errors are not concentrated in degraded audio either: the pooled error rate is 0.242 inside flagged segments and 0.258 outside, and wrongly transcribed words carry their fair share of sentiment importance (+0.006, CI [−0.010, 0.024]), so H2 is refuted. H3 (the text classifier is the bottleneck) is refuted under its pre-registered criterion because human transcripts raise Cardiff macro-F1 by a significant +0.022, but the size of that gain, against a gap of about 0.29 between Cardiff and the binary classifiers on the same transcripts (`Milestone2/T1_final/results/m2_t1_results.csv`), is the real answer to "why doesn't the speech model matter": the transcript explains at most 0.034 macro-F1. whisper-base on the same 50 clips has a lower WER (0.208 vs 0.243) yet the same Cardiff accuracy (0.74, `results/whisper-base/summary.json`) and 92% identical predicted labels (`Milestone2/AudioXAI/README.md`, computed from the two `aggregate.csv` files).

**Evidence.** The hypotheses and their refutation criteria were written before any test clip was read (`Milestone2/AudioXAI/HYPOTHESES.md`, committed in 016d798 at 04:49; test unlocked at 05:05). The tool (`audioxai` package with `explain`, `scan`, `m2`, `e2e`, `report` commands) computes, per clip, the exact log-mel input, an acoustic degradation timeline (AADD 3.1.0, registry model "v3", which `aadd info` describes as v3.1 with a Whisper-small encoder; 20 ms frames via `--resolution 0.02`; a frame is flagged at probability ≥ 0.5), token timestamps, four attribution methods (M1 gradient × input with SmoothGrad, M2 spectrogram occlusion, M3 Grad-CAM and M4 cross-attention, the last two time-only), word errors against the human transcript, and Integrated-Gradients sentiment importance per word; `results/aggregate.csv` has one row per clip, `report.html` is a self-contained offline page. Faithfulness: removing the top-attributed patches lowers the model's confidence faster than removing random patches (deletion AUC 1.426 vs 1.181, difference 0.245, CI [0.187, 0.303], 19 of 20 clips), but the **model-randomization sanity check fails**: after re-initializing the top two decoder layers the maps barely change (Spearman 0.916), and M1 correlates 0.78 with the input magnitude itself (`results/faithfulness.csv`). The maps therefore say more about where the energy is than about what the model computes, and the H1 verdict should be read with that caveat; M2 occlusion, which does not have this problem, gives the same focus ratio (0.997, CI includes 1).

**Extension with the spare time** (`Milestone2/AudioXAI/README.md`, section Extensions; summaries in `results/n300/summary.json`, `results/aventiq/summary.json`, `results/n100/summary.json`). On the brief's full 300-clip stratified sample with whisper-tiny, H1 is refuted again with a tighter interval: focus ratio 0.994, CI [0.966, 1.021], n = 214 clips with at least one flagged frame; only the music class (1.060) and hum (0.837) have per-class intervals that exclude 1. H2 is refuted again, with a twist worth reporting: the paired per-clip difference in word error rate (flagged minus clean) is −0.126 [−0.275, −0.017] on the 85 clips with both regions, while the pooled rates are 0.343 flagged versus 0.223 clean, so a few long, heavily flagged clips carry the pooled difference. AventIQ on the 50-clip sample: WER 0.210, Cardiff accuracy 0.78 (equal to its accuracy on human text), 96% of its predicted labels identical to whisper-tiny's, H1 refuted (0.991 [0.913, 1.060]), H2 refuted. M2 occlusion on the 100-clip sample agrees with M1 only weakly (per-token time-profile Spearman 0.167 [0.147, 0.186]) but gives the same focus ratio (0.989 [0.917, 1.069]). Deletion curves beat random on every run (300-clip AUC difference 0.201 [0.146, 0.251]); the randomization check fails on every run (Spearman 0.909–0.931).

**Limitations.** The committed headline sample is 50 clips (3 negative); the brief asked for 300 clips on three variants with M2 and segment-replacement on 100, and the extension reached 300 clips for whisper-tiny, 50 for whisper-base and AventIQ, M2 on 100 and segment replacement on 10. AADD was trained on synthetic degradations of read speech and flags about half of all 20 ms frames on this real interview audio (mean flagged fraction 0.505 on the test sample, `results/summary.json` key `mean_frac_flagged`), so "degraded" is an estimate. The per-clip attribution arrays (`.npz`, about 570 MB) are not committed.

**Answer to the project question in one paragraph.** The choice of speech-to-text model barely changes sentiment results because (1) even perfect, human transcripts raise the best classifier's macro-F1 by only 0.02–0.03, while switching classifiers moves it by about 0.3 (Phase 1); (2) word error rate and sentiment accuracy are not ordered the same way across models, and the two Whisper fine-tunes are effectively the same model (Phase 2); (3) editing the speech model to reduce its word error rate did not help, and slightly hurt, the sentiment result (Phase 3); and (4) the speech model's attention is not drawn to degraded audio, and transcription errors are neither concentrated in degraded audio nor on sentiment-bearing words, so the user's acoustic-quality hypothesis is refuted on this data, with the caveat that the gradient-based maps fail the randomization sanity check and the occlusion-based maps agree with them only weakly (Phase 4).
